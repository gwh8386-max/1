# The White Death

## SCENE 01: The last morning

> transition: fade up from black
L01: (low, close to the mic, unhurried) March 6th, 1940. [beat] A frozen forest near the Kollaa River, in eastern *Finland*.
L02: (quiet, steady) A small man in a white smock lies in the snow, his cheek pressed against an old bolt-action rifle.
L03: (tense, a touch faster) Somewhere across the clearing, a Soviet soldier sees him *first*. [pause]

## SCENE 04: The farmer
> visual: polaroid of young Simo pinned up; a hand-drawn farm, a cut-out of a hunter on skis; height ruler beside him stops at 1.52 m

L11: (warmer, unhurried) Simo was born in 1905 in Rautjärvi, a farming parish a few miles from the Russian *border*.
L12: (wry smile) He stood about five foot three. [beat] He farmed, he hunted, and he didn't talk *much*.
L13: (matter-of-fact) At seventeen he joined the Civil Guard, Finland's volunteer militia, and started winning *shooting* competitions. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L14: (dry) At paper targets, mostly. [beat] (quieter) That changed in the winter of *1939*. [pause]

## SCENE 07: Roads and forests
> visual: overhead map; a long red Soviet column of cut-out trucks and tanks jammed on a single pencil road; tiny white Finnish skiers slip out of the trees and cut the column into pieces, each piece circled like a woodpile

> transition: zoom out to the overhead map
L22: (explaining, unhurried) The Red Army has tanks, artillery and planes. [beat] What it doesn't have, out here, is *roads*.
L23: (steady) Its columns crawl along narrow forest tracks, miles long, with snow up to a man's *waist* on either side.
L24: (picking up speed) Finnish troops on skis come out of the trees, cut a column into pieces, and leave each piece to *freeze*.
L25: (dry, deadpan) The Finns call those pockets *motti*. [beat] It's their word for a neat stack of firewood. [KIỂM CHỨNG] [pause]

## SCENE 08: The other side of the clearing
> visual: point of view from the Soviet line: white trees, white ground, a thermometer cut-out dropping to -40; a clock face shows daylight shrinking to a thin slice
> text: -40° · DAYLIGHT: ~6 HRS
> sfx: teeth chattering, ice cracking, very low drone
> transition: freeze, color drains, camera turns around
L26: (close, second person) Now picture yourself on the *other* side of that clearing.
L27: (cold, slow) You're a Red Army conscript, maybe from somewhere far to the south, and it's forty degrees *below* zero. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L28: (tense, quiet) Daylight lasts about six hours. [beat] The trees are white, the ground is white, and every so often someone near you just *drops*.
L29: (whisper) You never hear where it came *from*. [long pause]

## SCENE 09: Why no scope
> visual: back on the board; a scope cut-out catches a sun flare, then fogs over with frost; a profile sketch of a head rising a few inches, red arrow marks the gap
> text: GLARE · FROST · HEAD UP 3–4"
> sfx: glint chime, frost crackle, marker squeak
> transition: whip-pan back to the board
L30: (curious, a touch brighter) Here's the thing. [beat] A scope would have helped him see. [beat] It also would have helped the enemy see *him*.
L31: (explaining, unhurried) The glass can catch the low winter sun and *flash*. [beat] And in that cold it can fog up or frost over.
L32: (matter-of-fact) A scope also makes you lift your head a few inches higher. [beat] (dry) Out there, a few inches is a *lot*.
L33: (wry smile) He knew about that flash because he spent his days looking for it on the *other* side.
L34: (dry, deadpan) So he used a Finnish copy of a Russian rifle, the old Mosin-Nagant, [beat] and pointed it back at the *Red Army*. [KIỂM CHỨNG] [pause]

## SCENE 10: The tricks
> visual: cross-section of a snow position; a hand pats snow into a bank, water poured over it turns to ice; a cartoon puff of breath gets crossed out; a sun and moon swing over the figure
> text: ICED SNOWBANK · NO BREATH · STILL

> transition: zoom through the map into a cross-section
L35: (low, conspiratorial) He had other *tricks*.
L36: (steady) He packed snow in front of his position and soaked it until it froze *hard*. [beat] That way the muzzle blast wouldn't kick up a puff of powder. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L37: (quieter) He kept snow in his mouth, so his breath wouldn't *steam* in the cold air. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L38: (slow, steady) And he lay *still*. [beat] For hours. [beat] Some days from before dawn until after dark.
L39: (dry, deadpan) Most of us can't get through a two-hour movie without checking our *phones*. [pause]

L47: (wry, flat) He gets up, and goes back to *work*. [long pause]

## SCENE 13: Who was counting?
> visual: three paper documents pinned side by side: a typed army report, a company ledger, a handwritten diary page; each with its own number; red string links them with question marks
> text: 219 (FEB 17, 1940) · ~500 · +200?
> sfx: typewriter, page turns, pen scratch
> transition: pan along the board
L48: (curious, slower) Which raises a question. [beat] Who was *counting*?
L49: (explaining) On February 17th, 1940, a Finnish army report credited him with two hundred nineteen confirmed *rifle* kills. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L50: (matter-of-fact) Later counts, including his own wartime diary, put the figure at around *five hundred*. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L51: (curious) That diary only became public in 2017, [beat] fifteen years after his *death*. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L52: (skeptical) Many books add roughly two hundred more with a submachine gun. [beat] Nobody could really *verify* those. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L53: (dry) At Kollaa, "confirmed" usually meant another soldier saw a man fall. [beat] Nobody walked into that clearing to *check*.
L54: (fair, measured) So the honest answer is, we don't know the *exact* number. [pause]
L55: (building) But take the lowest figure. [beat] Two hundred nineteen, in about eleven *weeks*.
L56: (incredulous) Lyudmila Pavlichenko, the Soviet Union's most famous sniper, was credited with three hundred nine over about a *year*. [KIỂM CHỨNG] [pause]

L70: (quieter, serious) Asked if he regretted the killing, he said he did what he was told, [beat] as well as he *could*. [KIỂM CHỨNG] [pause]

## SCENE 17: The clearing

> text: SIMO HÄYHÄ · 1905–2002 · title stamp THE WHITE DEATH
> sfx: wind, one distant rifle crack, silence
> transition: final pull-back, fade to white
L71: (warm, slower) Simo Häyhä died in 2002, at ninety-six, in a veterans' home in *Hamina*. [KIỂM CHỨNG]
L72: (quiet) In all those years, he never seemed very interested in the number everyone kept asking him *about*.
L73: (dry, then dropping) The soldiers across that clearing didn't need a number. [beat] (whisper) They just learned not to stand *up*. [long pause]
